# Assembly plan — unprinted prototype

This is the intended build order, not a report of a successful physical assembly. Inspect the exploded/hidden-front view and STEP before buying hardware. Listed screw lengths may need adjustment after printing; fastener-head clearances are not modeled.

## 1. Print the fit coupon

The four holes are 8.2, 8.4, 8.6 and 9.6 mm from left to right in the export. Compare them against the actual 8 mm ball and printed shaft. Do not force a tight fit; update clearance values and regenerate.

## 2. Dry-fit the lift

- The rear guard has four spacer pillars; these establish the separation from the front plate.
- Insert the shaft from the rear, with rear bush between the collar and rear guard.
- Fit the rotor on the D-keyed shaft. Check the direction of the flat.
- Add the front guard, front spacer, crank arm and shaft-retainer washer/cap.
- Use the M3 retainer screw into the printed shaft pilot. Leave the axial stack free rather than clamping the rotor solid.
- Add the handle knob with its M3 bolt, washers and nut. The knob should spin on the bolt.
- Four guard bolts hold the plates together. The lower two also pass through the stand blocks. Confirm actual engagement before tightening.

The nominal gap is 0.4 mm per rotor face. FDM first-layer expansion or warped plates can erase it; sand/test/revise rather than forcing the crank.

## 3. Mount to the base

- Attach the two lift stands through their base feet; M3 nuts sit in underside hex recesses.
- Bolt each matched-height track stand to the base.
- Attach the three troughs to their stands with M3 screws through mounting tabs into printed pilots.
- The track sequence is upper-return → side-return → lower-return. These have deliberately different heights.
- The funnel and sleeve are separate for printing. A small glue bead at their guard mating faces is the proposed attachment. Keep adhesive out of the bores. A screw-on redesign may be preferable after the first fit test.

## 4. Check transfers, one at a time

Rotate the empty crank through a full revolution first. The code checks several angular positions and the rotor envelope, but not fingers, fastener heads or all dynamic effects.

Align a lower pocket and add one 8 mm marble. Turn slowly in either direction to test loading. The intended route carries it to the upper exit, then down the three return tracks. The pocket face/guard window interaction can jam if the ball is only partially inserted; stop, don't force it.

The outlet, receiving cups, and funnel transfer lips are not dynamically proven. Test each independently at slow speed and revise if the ball catches, escapes or stalls. More than one ball may introduce jams and is outside the initial test scope.

## Planned hardware (not modeled)

- 2× M3×20 and 2× M3×30 guard bolts; lower pair pass through stands.
- 10× M3×12 base/stand bolts with underside M3 nuts.
- 6× M3×8 track-tab screws into printed pilots.
- 1× M3×12 shaft retainer screw into a pilot.
- 1× M3×35 handle bolt with nut/washers; trim length or use spacers after measuring.
- M3 washers as appropriate; do not overtighten into plastic.
- One nominal 8 mm steel/glass ball for initial testing, spare balls optional.
- Small adhesive quantity for funnel/sleeve mating faces.

These are prototype hardware allowances, not a validated purchase order. No automatic gate, second return route or motorization is included in v0.1.
