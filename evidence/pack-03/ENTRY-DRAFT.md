## Evidence-based writeup

This review extended the previous straight-track checks to the transfer regions. The three route segments were mapped in plan and height, then each corner was screened with an ideal rolling-speed and no-wall-hit projectile model. The model projected landing-centre offsets of about 21.6 mm and 19.5 mm. The nominal centre-safe radius of the receiving cup is 4.5 mm. This flags transfer risk; it is not proof that a ball escapes because the simplified model excludes rim impacts, friction and other contact effects.

An exact geometric sweep of the last track centreline and its extension found a different, concrete problem: the test sphere intersected the inlet funnel lip, with maximum overlap around 9.00 mm³. The earlier report had sampled only interior straight-track positions and did not establish this interface was clear.

Two separate lip-relief candidates were generated. The first reduced the collision but left a maximum sampled overlap of 2.68 mm³. Extending the clearance cut removed 37.55 mm³ of funnel material and reduced sampled overlap to 0.000 mm³. The candidate remains separate from the published machine: wall strength, retention and real capture behaviour are untested. The next step is a one-interface print and slow single-ball test, not an immediate claim of a working loop.
